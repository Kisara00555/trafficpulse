# 21 October 2026 - Part 8: a good prediction needs a sensible decision rule

## What was completed

Built a reproducible, explicitly hypothetical capacity-planning study. It uses the existing tree and baseline predictions, choosing capacity from either their point estimate or upper uncertainty bound. All four rules face the same 4,397 observed July-December 2017 hours. Nine combinations of resource size and shortfall penalty are reported; 19 missing hours stay excluded. No model was retuned and the final holdout was not opened.

## In simple English

Planning extra capacity can help avoid shortages, but unused capacity also has a cost. With an assumed block size of 500, using the tree's upper bound cuts hours with shortages from 840 to 118. It also raises average unused capacity from 254.3 to 653.4 vehicle-equivalents per hour. Whether that tradeoff is worthwhile depends on the penalties we assume.

The simple baseline has lower simulated cost in one coarse-capacity scenario even though its prediction error is worse. This is why we cannot call a model useful just because it is complex or has a better average error. We must check the decision it supports. These invented penalties are not money and do not prove real-world savings.
